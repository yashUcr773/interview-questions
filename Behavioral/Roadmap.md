# Behavioral & Leadership Interviews: Roadmap (0 → 1 → 100)

> Part of your [interview prep roadmaps](../README.md) · Pace: **~2 hrs/day** (small, steady slices) · Updated: Sep 2026

**Who this is for:** A senior engineer (~6 years) whose technical rounds may go well but who hasn't systematically prepared behavioral rounds. At senior level, behavioral and project deep-dive rounds often decide the **level** you're offered, not just whether you get an offer.

**What "done" looks like:** You have a bank of 12–15 well-structured, quantified stories mapped to every common theme. You can adapt them to any company's values (Amazon LPs, Google's Googleyness & Leadership, Meta's focus areas, Indian product companies' hiring-manager rounds, AI-first companies' mission questions). You can deliver each in 2–3 minutes and handle deep follow-ups without contradicting yourself.

**Pairs with:** [Backend HLD](../HLD/Roadmap.md) HLD-31 and [LLD](../LLD/Roadmap.md) LLD-26 (mock loops) · the project deep dive (BEH-08) reuses your system design vocabulary.

# Part A: 0 → 1 (Foundations)

### BEH-01 · What behavioral rounds test at senior level

**Time:** 2–3 h · **Level:** 0 → 1

**Why it matters:** Behavioral rounds aren't "soft". They're structured evaluations with rubrics, and at senior level they're often the leveling signal.

**Prerequisites**
- None

**What you'll learn**
- Round types: hiring manager, values/culture round, "bar raiser" (Amazon), "Googleyness & Leadership" (Google), behavioral/"past experience" rounds (Meta), project deep dive, cross-functional (with a PM or designer)
- What interviewers score: **scope** (task → project → team → org), **impact** (measurable outcomes), **ownership**, **collaboration and conflict**, **handling ambiguity**, **growth and self-awareness**, **leadership/influence**, **communication**
- How evidence is collected: specific past situations ("tell me about a time…"), follow-up probes ("what did *you* do?", "what would you do differently?")
- Level calibration: senior = leads projects end to end and influences the team; staff = influences multiple teams and sets technical direction
- Red flags: blaming others, vague "we", no metrics, no reflection, badmouthing employers, inconsistency between stories

**Hands-on**
1. Write down 3 behavioral questions you've been asked before and how you answered. Score each honestly against the dimensions above.

**Interview questions**
- (Meta) What do you think this interview is evaluating?

**Resources**
- [Hello Interview: Introduction to the behavioral interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/introduction) (primary)
- [Hello Interview blog: How behavioral interviews really work](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work)

**Pitfalls**
- Treating behavioral prep as something to do the night before.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] The round types you'll face
- [ ] The 8 evaluation dimensions
- [ ] How senior vs staff signals differ
- [ ] Common red flags

### BEH-02 · Career inventory & brag document

**Time:** 3–4 h · **Level:** 0 → 1

**Why it matters:** Six years of work contains dozens of great stories, but you'll forget most of them under pressure. Mining them systematically is the foundation of everything else.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-01

**What you'll learn**
- Building a **career inventory**: for each role and year, list projects, your role, the team size, technologies, outcomes (numbers), challenges, conflicts, mistakes, people you helped and decisions you drove
- Finding numbers: latency, cost, revenue, conversion, incidents, time saved, adoption, team velocity, hiring. Estimate honestly when exact data isn't available ("roughly 30%")
- A **brag document** you keep updated from now on
- Separating *your* contribution from the team's

**Hands-on**
1. Create `Behavioral/career-inventory.md` using the template below. Aim for 20+ raw entries across your 6 years; don't filter yet.
2. Start `Behavioral/brag-doc.md` and add to it weekly.

**Template: career inventory entry**
```
## <Project name> (<company>, <year>)
- Context: what the business/team needed
- My role: title, what I owned, team size
- What I did: 3–5 concrete actions (decisions, code, design, coordination)
- Outcome: metrics (before → after), business impact
- Hard parts: conflict / ambiguity / failure / tradeoffs
- People: who I influenced, mentored or disagreed with
- Tech: stack, architecture (useful for the deep dive)
- What I learned / would do differently
```

**Interview questions**
- What's the project you're most proud of?
- What's the most impactful thing you've done in your career?

**Resources**
- Julia Evans: [Get your work recognized: write a brag document](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/) (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Listing responsibilities ("I worked on payments") instead of actions and outcomes.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Your 20+ career inventory entries
- [ ] Metrics for at least 10 of them
- [ ] Your contribution vs the team's for each

### BEH-03 · Answer frameworks: STAR(L), headline-first, quantifying impact

**Time:** 2–3 h · **Level:** 0 → 1

**Why it matters:** Structure makes your answers easy to evaluate. Unstructured stories run long, bury the impact and invite skeptical follow-ups.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-02

**What you'll learn**
- **STAR + L:** Situation (brief, ~15%), Task (your responsibility), Action (~60%, specific, "I"), Result (quantified), Learning (what changed in how you work)
- **Headline first:** open with a one-sentence summary ("I led the migration that cut checkout latency by 40% despite pushback from two teams")
- Timing: 2–3 minutes per story; let the interviewer pull more detail
- "I" vs "we": credit the team, but make your actions explicit
- Quantifying: before → after, scale, and business relevance
- Adapting one story to different questions (conflict angle vs ownership angle)

**Hands-on**
1. Rewrite 3 inventory entries as STAR+L answers with a headline. Record yourself telling each; trim to under 3 minutes.

**Interview questions**
- Tell me about a time you improved a system significantly.

**Resources**
- [Tech Interview Handbook: Behavioral interviews](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) (primary)
- [Hello Interview: How to prepare for behavioral interviews](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/how-to-prepare)

**Pitfalls**
- 2 minutes of Situation, then 20 seconds of Action.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] STAR+L with proportions
- [ ] Headline-first delivery
- [ ] "I" vs "we" balance
- [ ] Quantifying results

### BEH-04 · Building your story bank

**Time:** 3–4 h · **Level:** 0 → 1

**Why it matters:** 12–15 versatile stories can answer nearly any behavioral question. A theme matrix ensures coverage and avoids reusing the same story in one loop.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-03

**What you'll learn**
- Choosing stories: recent (last 2–3 years preferred), senior in scope, with real stakes, a clear personal role and measurable results; include a few failures
- The **theme matrix** (below): each story covers 2–4 themes
- Story variety: technical leadership, cross-team work, people/mentoring, customer impact, failure/mistake, conflict with a peer, conflict with a manager, ambiguity, speed vs quality, AI/ML work if you have it
- Keeping details consistent (numbers, timelines, names of systems)

**Hands-on**
1. Pick 12–15 stories from your inventory; write each as STAR+L (bullets, not scripts) in `Behavioral/story-bank.md`.
2. Fill in the theme matrix; make sure every theme has at least 2 stories.

**Theme matrix (copy into your story bank)**

| Story | Conflict | Failure | Ownership | Ambiguity | Influence | Mentoring | Customer | Deadline | Tech leadership | Feedback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 … | | | | | | | | | | |
| S2 … | | | | | | | | | | |

**Interview questions**
- (Any question from BEH-06/BEH-07; use them to test coverage)

**Resources**
- [Tech Interview Handbook: behavioral question list](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-questions/) (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Only success stories. Failure and conflict stories are where self-awareness shows.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] 12–15 stories in STAR+L bullets
- [ ] Theme coverage ≥ 2 per theme
- [ ] Consistent numbers and timelines

### BEH-05 · "Tell me about yourself", "Why us?", "Why are you leaving?"

**Time:** 2–3 h · **Level:** 0 → 1

**Why it matters:** These open almost every round and set the interviewer's frame. A crisp, tailored narrative makes everything after it land better.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-04

**What you'll learn**
- **Tell me about yourself** (60–90 seconds): present (role, focus, a signature achievement) → past (2–3 highlights that build toward this role) → future (why this role is the logical next step). Tailor per role: full-stack vs frontend vs backend vs AI
- **Why this company/role:** specific product, technical, mission and growth reasons; show research
- **Why are you leaving / why now:** forward-looking, positive, honest; never badmouth
- Career narrative for a "generalist across 5 role types": a coherent through-line (e.g., "building user-facing products end to end, increasingly with AI")

**Hands-on**
1. Write and record three versions of "Tell me about yourself": full-stack/backend, frontend-heavy, and agentic/applied AI.
2. Write "Why us?" for 3 target companies.

**Interview questions**
- Tell me about yourself.
- Why do you want to join us?
- Why are you looking to leave your current role?
- Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?

**Resources**
- [Tech Interview Handbook: Self introduction](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/self-introduction/) (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Walking through your résumé chronologically for 5 minutes.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Your 3 tailored introductions (each ≤ 90 seconds)
- [ ] "Why us" for each target company
- [ ] A positive "why leaving" answer

# Part B: 1 → 100

## Level 1 → 10: Interview core

### BEH-06 · Core themes I: conflict, failure, feedback, pressure, prioritization

**Time:** 3–4 h · **Level:** 1 → 10

**Why it matters:** These five themes appear in nearly every behavioral loop. Interviewers look for maturity: data-driven disagreement, ownership of mistakes, openness to feedback and calm prioritization.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-04

**What you'll learn**
- **Conflict:** understand the other side, use data and prototypes, find the shared goal, escalate appropriately, preserve the relationship. Know the "disagree and commit" nuance
- **Failure/mistakes:** real stakes, your responsibility, what you did to fix it, a systemic change afterwards (postmortem, process, tests)
- **Feedback:** receiving critical feedback (and acting on it); giving hard feedback to a peer or junior
- **Pressure and deadlines:** scope negotiation, communicating risk early, tradeoffs (what you cut and why), sustainable pace
- **Prioritization:** frameworks (impact/effort, urgency, risk), saying no, aligning with stakeholders

**Hands-on**
1. For each theme, pick your best 2 stories; rehearse each aloud with a 2-minute timer; answer 3 follow-up probes for each ("what would you do differently?", "what did the other person think?", "what was the outcome for them?").

**Interview questions**
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or your manager. What happened?
- Tell me about a mistake that had real impact. What did you do?
- Tell me about the hardest feedback you've received.
- Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline. What did you cut?
- How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?

**Resources**
- *Crucial Conversations* (Patterson et al.): frameworks for conflict (primary)
- [Tech Interview Handbook: behavioral questions](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-questions/)

**Pitfalls**
- A "failure" that is really a humblebrag ("I worked too hard").

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] 2 rehearsed stories per theme
- [ ] Answers to the 3 standard follow-up probes for each
- [ ] The lesson and systemic change in each failure story

### BEH-07 · Core themes II: ownership, ambiguity, influence, mentoring, customer focus

**Time:** 3–4 h · **Level:** 1 → 10

**Why it matters:** These themes carry senior-level signals: acting beyond your remit, creating clarity, leading without authority and growing others.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-06

**What you'll learn**
- **Ownership:** noticing a problem outside your scope and driving it to resolution; on-call and incident ownership; long-term maintenance
- **Ambiguity:** turning vague goals into a plan (clarifying questions, prototypes, milestones, decision records)
- **Influence without authority:** persuading other teams with data, RFCs, prototypes; building alliances
- **Mentoring and growing others:** onboarding, code review culture, sponsoring juniors; measurable growth
- **Customer focus:** understanding user pain, going to the data, making tradeoffs in the user's favor
- **Innovation / simplification:** improving a process or architecture; removing complexity

**Hands-on**
1. Same drill as BEH-06: 2 stories per theme, rehearsed with probes.
2. Record one full "mock" set of 5 random questions from BEH-06/BEH-07; watch it back and note filler words, pacing and missing metrics.

**Interview questions**
- Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your responsibilities.
- Tell me about a project that started with unclear requirements.
- Tell me about a time you convinced another team to change their approach.
- How have you helped someone on your team grow?
- Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer or user.

**Resources**
- *The Staff Engineer's Path* (Tanya Reilly): influence and ambiguity chapters (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Influence stories where you "just escalated to the manager".

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] 2 rehearsed stories per theme
- [ ] Your recorded mock and the fixes from it

### BEH-08 · The project deep dive

**Time:** 4–5 h · **Level:** 1 → 10

**Why it matters:** Many companies (and most senior loops) ask you to walk through a significant project for 30–45 minutes, drilling into architecture, decisions, your role and outcomes. It tests depth, ownership and judgment at once.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-04; HLD Part A (to explain architecture clearly)

**What you'll learn**
- Choosing 2 projects: one deeply technical (architecture/scale) and one with leadership/cross-team complexity; ideally one AI-related if targeting AI roles
- Structure: problem and business context → constraints → architecture (draw it) → key decisions and alternatives considered → your specific role → challenges and how you solved them → results (metrics) → what you'd do differently
- Preparing for drill-downs: data model, scale numbers, failure modes, why not X, testing, rollout, monitoring, team dynamics
- Handling confidentiality: abstract names, share approximate numbers
- Consistency with your résumé and other stories

**Hands-on**
1. For both projects: draw the architecture diagram (Excalidraw), write a one-page brief, and list 15 likely drill-down questions with answers.
2. Do a 30-minute recorded walkthrough of each; have a friend (or AI, after your attempt) ask follow-up questions.

**Interview questions**
- Walk me through the most technically complex project you've worked on.
- Why did you choose this architecture? What alternatives did you consider?
- What would you change if you did it again?
- What was your specific contribution vs the team's?
- How did you measure success?

**Resources**
- Your own design docs and PRs (primary)
- [HLD roadmap](../HLD/Roadmap.md) HLD-01 (structure your explanation like a system design)

**Pitfalls**
- Being unable to explain decisions you weren't personally involved in. Say so honestly, and explain what you'd have chosen.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] 2 projects with diagrams and one-page briefs
- [ ] 15 drill-down answers per project
- [ ] Alternatives and tradeoffs for each key decision

### BEH-09 · Company-specific preparation

**Time:** 4–5 h · **Level:** 1 → 10

**Why it matters:** Each company frames behavioral evaluation differently. Mapping your story bank to their language is high-leverage.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-06, BEH-07

**What you'll learn**
- **Amazon:** the 16 Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession; Ownership; Invent and Simplify; Are Right, A Lot; Learn and Be Curious; Hire and Develop the Best; Insist on the Highest Standards; Think Big; Bias for Action; Frugality; Earn Trust; Dive Deep; Have Backbone, Disagree and Commit; Deliver Results; Strive to be Earth's Best Employer; Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility). Every round asks LP questions, and a bar raiser is present. Prepare 2 stories per LP (stories can overlap)
- **Google:** "Googleyness & Leadership": collaboration, humility, handling ambiguity, doing the right thing, leadership without authority; hypothetical ("what would you do if…") as well as past-behavior questions
- **Meta:** behavioral rounds probing themes like conflict, growth, ambiguity, driving impact and communication; strong emphasis on scope and impact for leveling
- **Microsoft and other big tech:** growth mindset, collaboration, customer focus
- **Indian product companies** (Flipkart, Swiggy, Razorpay, PhonePe, Meesho, Atlassian India, Uber India…): hiring-manager round mixing behavioral, project deep dive and culture fit; sometimes a "bar raiser"-style round; questions about notice period, relocation and compensation expectations
- **AI-first companies and labs:** mission alignment, views on AI safety and responsible deployment, comfort with fast iteration and ambiguity, evidence of shipping and evaluating AI features
- **Startups:** ownership, breadth, speed, dealing with scarce resources, founder-style questions ("what would you build first?")
- Research each company: values pages, engineering blogs, recent launches, Glassdoor/Blind/LeetCode discuss for interview reports (take with a grain of salt)

**Hands-on**
1. Build an Amazon LP → story mapping table (2 stories per LP).
2. For your top 5 target companies: a one-page brief (values, products, recent news, likely questions, your "why us", 3 questions to ask).

**Interview questions**
- (Amazon) Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager and committed anyway.
- (Amazon) Tell me about a time you had to dive deep to find the root cause.
- (Google) Tell me about a time you had to work with a difficult stakeholder. What would you do if you saw a teammate cutting corners?
- (AI-first) Why do you want to work on AI? How do you think about the risks of the systems you build?
- (Startup) What would you do in your first 90 days here?

**Resources**
- [Amazon Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) (primary for Amazon)
- [Hello Interview: behavioral guides](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/introduction)
- Company careers and engineering-culture pages for each target

**Pitfalls**
- Reciting company values back without stories that demonstrate them.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] The LP → story mapping
- [ ] Company briefs for your top 5
- [ ] How each company's behavioral round is structured

## Level 10 → 50: Senior depth

### BEH-10 · Senior & staff signals

**Time:** 3–4 h · **Level:** 10 → 50

**Why it matters:** With 6 years of experience you're likely being evaluated for senior (or staff) levels. The same story can signal SDE-2 or Senior depending on how you frame scope and influence.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-08

**What you'll learn**
- Scope ladder: task → feature → project → multi-team initiative → org-wide direction; framing your stories at the highest *honest* scope
- Senior signals: owning projects end to end, technical decisions with tradeoffs, mentoring, improving team processes, handling incidents, working with product and design
- Staff signals: setting technical direction across teams, writing influential RFCs, resolving cross-team conflicts, raising the bar for engineering quality, identifying problems no one asked you to solve, sponsoring others
- Archetypes of staff engineers (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand)
- Demonstrating judgment: when you chose *not* to build something; managing tech debt strategically
- Leveling conversations: how to ask about level and how it's decided

**Hands-on**
1. Re-frame your top 5 stories with explicit scope, influence and decision-making language; compare the before and after recordings.
2. Write a "leveling case" for yourself: 5 bullets of senior/staff evidence.

**Interview questions**
- Tell me about a technical decision you made that affected multiple teams.
- How do you decide what to work on?
- Tell me about a time you raised the engineering bar on your team.

**Resources**
- Will Larson: [StaffEng](https://staffeng.com/) and the book *Staff Engineer* (primary)
- *The Staff Engineer's Path* (Tanya Reilly)

**Pitfalls**
- Inflating scope beyond what follow-ups can support.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] The scope ladder and where your stories sit
- [ ] Senior vs staff signals
- [ ] Your leveling case

### BEH-11 · Hard & uncomfortable questions

**Time:** 2–3 h · **Level:** 10 → 50

**Why it matters:** Awkward questions (gaps, layoffs, weaknesses, conflicts with managers) are where candidates get defensive. Prepared, honest, forward-looking answers build trust.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-05

**What you'll learn**
- Weaknesses: a real one, what you're actively doing about it, and evidence of progress
- Career gaps, layoffs, short tenures: brief, factual, forward-looking
- "Tell me about a time you were wrong" / "a decision you regret"
- Ethical dilemmas: pressure to ship something unsafe, data misuse, cutting corners
- Managing up: disagreeing with leadership direction
- Handling a low performer on the team
- "Why haven't you been promoted?" / "Why so long at one company?"
- Salary expectations asked early (see BEH-14)

**Hands-on**
1. Write and rehearse answers for each item that applies to you; get a trusted peer to ask them unexpectedly.

**Interview questions**
- What's your biggest weakness?
- Tell me about a decision you regret.
- Tell me about a time you were asked to do something you thought was wrong.
- Why did you leave your previous company after only a year?

**Resources**
- [Tech Interview Handbook: behavioral interviews](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Over-explaining. Keep it short, honest, and pivot to what you learned.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Your weakness answer with evidence of progress
- [ ] Answers for any gaps or short tenures
- [ ] An ethical-dilemma story or approach

### BEH-12 · Questions to ask & reverse interviewing

**Pitfalls**
- "No, I think you covered everything."

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**

- [ ] Red flags you'll listen for

### BEH-13 · Mock practice & feedback loop

**Time:** 6–8 h (ongoing) · **Level:** 10 → 50

**Why it matters:** Delivery (conciseness, confidence, structure) improves only through spoken practice with feedback.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-06, BEH-07

**What you'll learn**
- Solo recordings (video) reviewed against the rubric below
- Peer mocks (exchange with friends preparing for interviews); paid mock platforms if useful
- AI-assisted practice: have an AI play interviewer and probe follow-ups *after* you've prepared your stories
- A post-interview retro habit: questions asked, what went well, what to fix

**Hands-on**
1. One behavioral mock per week during active interviewing (alternate solo recording and peer mock).
2. Keep `Behavioral/mock-log.md`.

**Rubric (score 1–4 each)**

| Dimension | 4 looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Structure | Headline first; clear STAR; 2–3 minutes |
| Specificity | Concrete actions with "I"; real details |
| Impact | Quantified results tied to the business or users |
| Scope | Appropriate to the target level |
| Self-awareness | Honest reflection; lessons applied later |
| Handling probes | Consistent, detailed answers to follow-ups |
| Presence | Calm, concise, few filler words |

**Interview questions**
- A random set of 5 from BEH-05 to BEH-11 each session

**Resources**
- [Hello Interview: How to prepare for behavioral interviews](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/overview/how-to-prepare) (primary)

**Pitfalls**
- Memorizing scripts word for word. You'll sound robotic; use bullets instead.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Your rubric average over the last 4 mocks (target ≥ 3)
- [ ] Your top 2 delivery improvements

## Level 50 → 100: Expert

### BEH-14 · Offers, leveling & negotiation

**Time:** 2–3 h · **Level:** 50 → 100

**Why it matters:** Negotiation often changes total compensation by 10–30% and can change your level. It's a learnable skill.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-10

**What you'll learn**
- Negotiation principles: get everything in writing, don't give numbers first if you can avoid it, enthusiasm + leverage, negotiate the whole package, be polite and patient
- Compensation components: base, variable/bonus, joining/sign-on bonus, RSUs (vesting schedules, refreshers), ESOPs at startups (strike price, vesting, cliff, liquidity/buybacks), relocation, benefits
- **India-specific:** CTC vs in-hand, notice periods (often 60–90 days) and notice-period buyouts, counter-offers from your current employer, "expected CTC" questions early in the process
- Level negotiation: asking how the level was decided; providing evidence for a higher level
- Competing offers: timing your pipeline so offers arrive close together
- Researching comp: levels.fyi, peers, recruiters
- Deciding between offers: role scope, team, manager, growth, learning (especially AI exposure), comp, stability

**Hands-on**
1. Build an offer comparison spreadsheet template (4-year total comp, vesting, level, team, growth factors).
2. Role-play a negotiation call with a friend.

**Interview questions**
- What are your salary expectations?
- Do you have other offers?

**Resources**
- Haseeb Qureshi: [Ten Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer](https://haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer/) (primary)
- [levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) (compensation data, including India)

**Pitfalls**
- Accepting the first verbal offer on the call.
- Revealing your current CTC as an anchor when you don't have to.

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Negotiation principles
- [ ] All compensation components, including ESOP terms
- [ ] India-specific factors (notice period, buyouts, counter-offers)
- [ ] Your offer comparison framework

### BEH-15 · Remote, international & startup-specific preparation

**Time:** 1–2 h · **Level:** 50 → 100

**Why it matters:** Remote and international roles assess written communication and async collaboration. Startups assess ownership and breadth.

**Prerequisites**
- BEH-09

**What you'll learn**
- Remote: async communication, written design docs, time-zone overlap, self-management, building relationships remotely; take-home assignments and written exercises
- International: cultural context in answers (directness, credit-sharing), visa and relocation discussions
- Startups: breadth stories, shipping with minimal resources, product sense, equity understanding

**Hands-on**

**Interview questions**

- Tell me about a time you shipped something important with very limited resources.

**Resources**

**Pitfalls**

**Checklist: you should now be able to explain**
- [ ] Your async-collaboration stories
- [ ] Startup-specific framing of your experience

# Question bank (by theme)

| Theme | Sample questions |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict | Disagreed with a peer? With your manager? A time you had to push back on product? |
| Failure | A project that failed? A production incident you caused? A missed deadline? |
| Feedback | Hardest feedback received? Feedback you gave that was hard to deliver? |
| Ownership | Went beyond your role? Fixed something nobody owned? Long-term ownership of a system? |
| Ambiguity | Started with unclear requirements? Made a decision with incomplete data? |
| Influence | Convinced another team? Drove adoption of a tool or practice? Changed a senior person's mind? |
| Mentoring | Helped someone grow? Onboarded a new hire? Improved code review culture? |
| Customer | Went above and beyond for users? Used data to understand users? |
| Deadlines | Delivered under pressure? Cut scope wisely? |
| Tech leadership | Architecture decision with tradeoffs? Tech debt strategy? Led a migration? |
| Innovation | Simplified a complex system? Introduced a new technology successfully (e.g., AI)? |
| Ethics | Asked to do something you felt was wrong? Handled user data responsibly? |
